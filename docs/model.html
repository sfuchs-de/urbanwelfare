Sufficient-Statistics Framework

urbanwelfare implements the five-step sufficient-statistics framework of Allen, Fuchs, Ganapati, Graziano, Madera, and Montoriol-Garriga (2026) in a two-sector small open city.

1. Household & Producer Envelopes (Propositions 2.1–2.2)

Around an initial interior optimum with fixed outside-good price ($p_0 = 1$) and fixed short-run housing rents, Shephard’s lemma and workplace earnings optimization yield first-order money-metric resident welfare in origin $n$:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}EV_{nt}}{v_n^0} = \mathrm{d}\log v_{nt} - \sum_{i=1}^N s_{ni}\,\mathrm{d}\log p_{it}, \qquad s_{ni} = \frac{E_{ni}^{R,0}}{v_n^0}.$$

Because transaction datasets record nominal sales $y_{it} = p_{it} q_{it}$ rather than unit prices $p_{it}$, competitive firms combining fixed local factors with flexible traded inputs at fixed external prices satisfy the producer envelope $\mathrm{d}G_{it} = y_i^0\,\mathrm{d}\log p_{it}$. Defining the marginal price share $\kappa_i^S \equiv \mathrm{d}G_i / \mathrm{d}y_i = (1 + \varepsilon_i^S)^{-1} \in (0,1]$ gives:

$$\mathrm{d}\log p_{it} = \kappa_i^S\,\mathrm{d}\log y_{it}, \qquad \mathrm{d}\log v_{nt}^{\mathrm{prod}} = \sum_{i=1}^N H_{ni}^m \frac{y_i^0}{v_n^0}\kappa_i^S\,\mathrm{d}\log y_{it}.$$

2. Spatial Network Propagation (Proposition 2.3a)

Let $\Gamma_{v,ni} = H_{ni}^m G_i^0 / v_n^0$ map destination factor income to residential origins, and $\Omega^v_{in} = E_{ni}^{R,0} / y_i^0$ map origin income back to commercial destinations. One earning-and-shopping round is $M = \Omega^v \Gamma_v$. Because spending leaks into flexible traded inputs, housing/saving, outside traded goods, and external factor owners, $\rho(M) < 1$ and the infinite-round exposures are:

$$\mathbf{F}_t^y = (I - M)^{-1}\mathbf{d}_t = \sum_{h=0}^{\infty} M^h \mathbf{d}_t, \qquad \mathbf{F}_t^v = \Gamma_v \mathbf{F}_t^y, \qquad d_{it} = \frac{E_i^{\mathrm{ext},0}}{y_i^0} h_{it}.$$

3. Bilateral Spending Regression (Proposition 2.4)

Under local homotheticity and spatial IIA relative to the outside good $i = 0$, basket-centering destination exposures $\widetilde{x}_{nit} = x_{it} - \sum_{j=1}^N s_{nj}^{NH} x_{jt}$ ($\sum_{j=0}^N s_{nj}^{NH}\widetilde{x}_{njt} = 0$) separates relative origin income growth from destination expenditure switching in a single HDFE regression with pair ($\alpha_{ni}$) and period ($\delta_t$) fixed effects:

$$\log E^R_{nit} = \alpha_{ni} + \delta_t + \underbrace{a_O h_{nt} + b_v F^v_{nt}}_{\text{origin income}} + \underbrace{a_D \widetilde{h}_{nit} + b_D \widetilde{F}^y_{nit}}_{\text{expenditure switching}} + \varepsilon_{nit}.$$

Differentiating goods-market clearing then recovers destination sales growth $\mathrm{d}\log \widehat{y}_{it} = d_{it} + \sum_n \Omega^v_{in}\,\mathrm{d}\log \widehat{E}^R_{nit}$, and matching $\mathrm{d}\log \widehat{v}_{nt}$ against $\sum_{i \in g} H_{ni}^m(y_i^0/v_n^0)\,\mathrm{d}\log \widehat{y}_{it}$ identifies sector marginal price shares $\widehat{\kappa}_g^S$.

4. Open-City Level Anchor & Terms of Trade (Proposition 2.5)

Time fixed effects $\delta_t$ absorb the city-wide income shift $c_t$. In a small open city with fixed outside-sector factor earnings ($\mathrm{d}G^O = 0$), aggregate resident income growth must equal local factor payments accruing to residents:

$$\sum_{n \in \mathcal{R}} v_n^0 (\mathrm{d}\log \widehat{v}_{nt} + c_t) = \sum_{n \in \mathcal{R}}\sum_{i=1}^N H_{ni}^m y_i^0 \kappa_i^S (\mathrm{d}\log \widehat{y}_{it} + \Omega_i^v c_t) \implies \widehat{c}_t = -\frac{\psi_t}{1 - \vartheta}.$$

Summing anchored $\mathrm{d}EV_{nt}$ over all city residents cancels internal resident-to-resident sales and yields the open-city aggregate terms-of-trade identity verified automatically by recover_welfare():

$$\sum_{n \in \mathcal{R}} \mathrm{d}EV_{nt} = \sum_{i=1}^N E_i^{\mathrm{nonres},0}\,\mathrm{d}\log p_{it} - \sum_{i=1}^N (1 - H_i^{\mathcal{R}}) y_i^0\,\mathrm{d}\log p_{it}.$$
