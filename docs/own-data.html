Use Your Own Data

urbanwelfare is designed for modern intra-city transaction and mobility datasets—such as payment cards (CaixaBank, BBVA, Affinity, Facteus, Nets), instant payments (Pix), retail scanner panels, or foot-traffic gravity panels (SafeGraph/Advan) paired with commuting records (Census LEHD/LODES, register commuting, or mobile-device home–work matrices).

Option A: Start from three tidy CSV tables

If you have raw panel tables rather than pre-balanced equilibrium matrices, pass them directly to urban_from_tables() or run_urban_welfare():

TableRequired ColumnsEconomic Meaning
bilateral_panel origin, dest, time, spending Resident expenditure $E^R_{nit}$ by home neighborhood $n$, commercial destination $i$, and period $t$.
commuting origin, dest, flow Initial workplace payroll $W_{ni}^0$ (or factor-claim share $H_{ni}^m \in [0,1]$) linking destination $i$ to home $n$.
external_demand dest, time, external_spending (or h) Shocked external/visitor/event demand $E^{\mathrm{ext}}_{it}$ (or log shift $h_{it}$). 
built <- urban_from_tables(
  bilateral_panel = read.csv("resident_card_panel.csv"),
  commuting       = read.csv("lehd_commuting.csv"),
  external_demand = read.csv("visitor_spending.csv"),
  base_time       = "2018-01",
  origins         = read.csv("tract_populations_incomes.csv") # optional
)
summary(built$account)

Option B: Noisy card coverage & IPF balancing

Raw card panels rarely capture 100% of household expenditure. In urban_account(), setting balance_method = "ipf" applies Iterative Proportional Fitting (RAS) to align noisy bilateral card shares with official origin non-housing budgets $\nu_n(1 - s_{n0}^{NH})v_n^0$ and destination consumer sales totals $y_i^0 - E_i^{\mathrm{ext},0}$ before constructing $(\Gamma_v, \Omega^v, M)$.

Option C: Surrounding metropolitan commuters

Cities trade daily with surrounding metropolitan municipalities $\mathcal{M}$: suburban commuters earn wages at city workplaces and shop at city commercial hubs, but city policy objectives evaluate welfare over core city residents $\mathcal{R}$. Include suburban origins directly in origins with is_city_resident = FALSE:

  • M = Omega_v %*% Gamma_v automatically incorporates metropolitan earning-and-shopping feedback $\Omega_R\Gamma_R + \Omega_M\Gamma_M$.
  • recover_welfare() and optimal_spatial_tax() automatically restrict welfare weights, lump-sum tax rebates, and $\widehat{c}_t$ factor-income matching to core city residents (is_city_resident == TRUE).

Option D: Secure data enclaves

When raw bilateral card records reside inside an air-gapped bank or census enclave:

  1. Install urbanwelfare inside the enclave (zero external C++ system libraries required—only standard R packages Rcpp, Matrix, fixest, and ggplot2).
  2. Or export only the aggregated initial matrices $(E^{R,0}, H^m)$, exposures, and the 4-element slope vector $(a_O, b_v, a_D, b_D)$ + $4 \times 4$ clustered covariance matrix, then reconstruct the fit outside the enclave via bilateral_from_estimates():
fit <- bilateral_from_estimates(
  coefficients = c(a_O = -0.0059, b_v = 1.2176, a_D = -0.1200, b_D = -0.3066),
  exposures    = exposures,
  vcov         = exported_vcov_4x4,
  n_obs        = 3260707L
)
welf <- recover_welfare(fit, acct, period = "201807")
